## Step 2: Evaluate Educational Value

### None — Reporting without meaningful teaching

Assign **None** when the text mainly announces, reports, advertises, entertains, or expresses opinions without providing substantial explanations or knowledge.

Typical examples:
- Simple news reports about events, politics, sports, or public activities.
- Announcements of competitions, concerts, conferences, and educational events.
- Reports about schools, universities, or scientific conferences that do not explain the underlying subject.
- Advertising, promotional material, product listings, and personal updates.
- Everyday conversations and entertainment discussions.

Important: A text can contain factual information and still be **None**. Reporting who, what, where, and when is not sufficient evidence of educational value.

### Minimal — Mostly reporting, with some useful background

Assign **Minimal** when the text is primarily non-educational but includes a small amount of meaningful factual, historical, technical, or cultural context.

Typical examples:
- News articles containing brief historical explanations.
- Event reports describing the background of a tradition or cultural practice.
- Travel writing containing basic facts about a location.
- Product descriptions mentioning a few technical characteristics.
- Institutional articles providing limited background about an industry or subject.

The key distinction from None is the presence of useful background knowledge beyond the immediate event or announcement.

The key distinction from Basic is that explaining the subject is not the text's primary purpose.

### Basic — Primarily informative or explanatory

Assign **Basic** when the main purpose of the text is to describe a subject, communicate knowledge, or explain a concept, but the treatment is relatively introductory, superficial, fragmented, or limited in depth.

Typical examples:
- Simple Wikipedia-style biographies.
- Basic encyclopedia entries about places, people, historical events, or scientific concepts.
- Introductory explanations of technology, religion, culture, geography, or history.
- Short educational articles without substantial detail.
- Informative literary narratives and cultural descriptions.
- Simple classroom exercises or learning materials with limited explanation.

A text may contain technical terminology or advanced subject matter and still be Basic if it mainly summarizes or lists facts rather than explaining them in depth.

Use Basic as the default for ordinary coherent encyclopedia-style articles.

### Good — Substantial and useful knowledge

Assign **Good** when the text provides substantial educational or informational value through detailed, coherent explanations, organized presentation, or practical instruction.

Typical examples:
- Detailed encyclopedia or reference articles that go beyond introductory descriptions.
- Scientific or technical articles explaining concepts, mechanisms, or processes.
- Well-developed historical or cultural explanations.
- Structured lessons and educational materials.
- Detailed recipes, gardening advice, religious instructions, and practical tutorials.
- Informative biographies that explain significant achievements, contributions, or historical context.

Formal instructional features such as exercises, learning objectives, or step-by-step procedures are helpful but not required.

The text must provide more than an ordinary factual summary. Look for meaningful depth, explanations, connections between ideas, detailed descriptions, or knowledge that readers can apply.

Do not assign Good simply because the text is long, factual, technical, or written in an academic style.

### Excellent — Exceptionally deep and comprehensive educational content

Assign **Excellent** when the text is an exceptionally valuable resource for learning, with substantial depth, extensive explanations, strong organization, and comprehensive coverage of its subject.

Typical examples:
- Detailed educational lessons with clear objectives, explanations, and activities.
- In-depth scientific, technical, or academic articles.
- Comprehensive reference material explaining complex concepts and their relationships.
- Expert-level guides with meaningful examples or practical applications.
- Extensive educational treatments of history, language, literature, culture, or social science.

Excellent does not require a formal classroom format. A deeply informative academic or reference article can qualify.

However, advanced vocabulary, specialized terminology, and document length alone are insufficient.

Reserve Excellent for texts that offer unusually substantial learning value compared with typical informative articles.

## Step 3: Resolve Ambiguous Cases

Apply these distinctions carefully:

- **None vs. Minimal:** Does the text contain meaningful background knowledge beyond reporting an event?
- **Minimal vs. Basic:** Is explaining or describing a subject the primary purpose of the text?
- **Basic vs. Good:** Does the text provide substantial explanation, depth, organization, or practical usefulness beyond an introductory overview?
- **Good vs. Excellent:** Is the educational treatment exceptionally comprehensive, detailed, and insightful rather than merely useful and well-written?

Additional rules:

- A simple news article is generally None, even when it concerns science, education, or culture.
- A news article with incidental historical or contextual information may be Minimal.
- A typical Wikipedia-style article is generally Basic.
- A detailed encyclopedia article with substantial explanations may be Good.
- An exceptionally comprehensive and deep reference article may be Excellent.
- A recipe with clear, useful instructions may be Good.
- A structured school lesson may be Good or Excellent depending on its depth and completeness.
- Fiction and literary texts should be evaluated for the knowledge and learning value they actually provide, not automatically assigned a high score because they are literature.
- Do not confuse the importance of a topic with the educational quality of the extract.
- Evaluate only the supplied extract. Do not assume missing sections contain additional educational content.
